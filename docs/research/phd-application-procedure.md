# PhD application procedure, end to end (Taiwanese applicant, Fall 2027, US + Europe)

Research note. Access dates below are **2026-10-12** unless stated otherwise. This file answers the *generic procedural* question — what the application/visa machinery concretely involves — not which schools or professors to target. It is written to sit alongside `docs/phd-discussion-handoff-context.md`, which has the field/program-choice content.

Legend: **[FACT]** = directly sourced from a primary/official page, with citation. **[INFER]** = my synthesis across sources, not stated verbatim anywhere. **[REC]** = a recommendation/judgment call, not a fact.

---

## 0. The big caveat up front

There is no single "PhD application procedure." It forks hard along at least three axes:
- **US vs. UK vs. continental Europe** — different admission models entirely (committee-based vs. PI-funded-position-based vs. structured-program-based).
- **Department/field** — even within one university, policies on GRE, writing samples, and POI contact diverge by department.
- **Funding mechanism** in Europe — whether the PhD is a university-admitted doctorate (apply like a degree) or a funded position/studentship (apply like a job posting) changes almost everything about deadlines, documents, and POI contact.

Sections 1 and 2 below are organized to make this forking explicit rather than averaging it away.

---

## 1. Generic document/component checklist

### 1.1 Statement of Purpose / Personal Statement
**[INFER]** Required everywhere, but naming and emphasis differ: US programs typically call it a "Statement of Purpose" (research-focused); some ask additionally for a "Personal History/Diversity Statement." European structured programs and UK PhDs often want a "Research Proposal" (expected to engage with a specific supervisor's work) rather than a general SOP. This is consistent with the handoff doc's own plan of two SOP variants (medical wave inversion / photonic inverse design) — confirm per-program whether a free-form SOP or a project-specific proposal is wanted, since these are different documents.

### 1.2 Letters of recommendation (LORs)
**[FACT]** Typical count is **2–3**, submitted online: the applicant enters recommenders' names/emails in the portal, and each recommender receives an automated link to upload a letter directly — Boston University states 3 required for all PhD programs ([BU GRS PhD/MFA LOR page](https://www.bu.edu/cas/?p=65284), accessed 2026-10-12); Cornell's graduate school: typically 2–3 depending on field ([search synthesis, Cornell grad school](https://mdphd.weill.cornell.edu/how-many-letters-recommendation-are-required), accessed 2026-10-12). **[INFER]** Exact count and portal mechanics are program-specific; verify per school. The handoff doc already has 3 named recommenders (MS advisor, NYCU PET PI, ASUS researcher), which covers the typical requirement.

### 1.3 Transcripts
**[FACT]** For a non-English degree (Taiwan: NTU/NYCU), US and European schools generally want **both** the original-language official transcript **and** an English translation; whether the translation must be "certified" (and by whom) varies by school:
- Boston University requires a **direct, certified translation**, and explicitly states a WES-type credential evaluation does **not** substitute for an official transcript ([BU translation/notary guide](https://www.bu.edu/cas/files/2016/10/UPDATED-Notary-and-Translation-Guide.pdf), accessed 2026-10-12).
- William & Mary requires certified, literal English translations, accepting a WES ICAP package only as an optional substitute for *delivering* official transcripts, not for translation itself ([W&M international applicant requirements](https://www.wm.edu/as/graduate/admission/international-appl-req-and-inst/), accessed 2026-10-12).
- LSU's graduate school does **not** require a third-party credential evaluation but does require certified English translations when the issuing institution doesn't supply one ([search synthesis citing LSU grad school policy](https://lists.latech.edu/pipermail/la-cgs/2026-March/000711.html), accessed 2026-10-12, dated March 2026 — relatively current).
- Some schools (e.g., Cal State LA, Southern University) require a **course-by-course credential evaluation** from an approved agency (WES/IERF/ECE) in addition to or instead of translation ([Cal State LA](https://www.calstatela.edu/admissions/ierfwes), accessed 2026-10-12).

**[INFER]** Net practical effect for an NTU/NYCU applicant: most transcripts issued by NTU/NYCU already come with an official English-language transcript from the registrar (common at Taiwanese universities), which satisfies many US schools' "certified translation" requirement without a separate agency. **A minority of schools additionally require a WES/ECE-style evaluation** — this must be checked per program, since it adds weeks and a fee (~US$100–200) if required and not already planned for.

### 1.4 CV/resume
**[INFER]** Universally required for PhD applications (unlike many undergrad/professional-master's apps); no special Taiwan-specific requirement found. Standard content: education, publications (the IEEE AP-S 2026 paper and MS thesis matter here), research experience, teaching experience, skills.

### 1.5 Writing sample
**[INFER]** Common in humanities/social-science PhDs; **not standard** for engineering/applied-physics PhDs based on the US program pages reviewed (none of the engineering admissions pages surfaced in search asked for one). Some programs optionally accept a "research sample" (e.g., a paper or thesis chapter) in lieu — the MS thesis or the AP-S paper could serve this role if a program asks. **[REC]** Don't produce one unless a specific target program's page asks for it.

### 1.6 Standardized tests (GRE / TOEFL / IELTS), current status as of 2026

**GRE General Test:**
- **[FACT]** Current fee: **US$249** (effective Aug 1, 2026, "subject to change"; China-specific pricing differs, Taiwan is bucketed under "all other areas of the world") ([ETS GRE fees page](https://www.ets.org/gre/revised_general/register/fees/), accessed 2026-10-12).
- **[FACT]** Fee Reduction Voucher (brings fee to ~US$100) exists but is **restricted to US citizens/permanent residents** testing in the US/Puerto Rico/Guam/USVI (financial-need, unemployed, or Peace Corps categories), or to members of specific partner programs (Gates Millennium, Rangel Fellowship) — **a Taiwan-based, non-US-citizen applicant does not qualify** for the standard voucher routes ([ETS GRE fee reduction program](https://www.ets.org/gre/revised_general/register/fees/), accessed 2026-10-12).
- **[FACT]** Requirement is increasingly optional/department-dependent even within one university. Example: UW Bioengineering PhD page is headlined "GRE Now Optional" ([UW BioE PhD admissions](https://bioe.uw.edu/academic-programs/phd/phd-admissions), accessed 2026-10-12). Policy is **not uniform** — must be checked per department, not per university.
- **[FACT]** The GRE Physics Subject Test still exists (moved to computer-delivery, shortened format starting Sept 2023) — it was **not** discontinued, unlike the GRE Chemistry Subject Test, which ETS did discontinue after April 2023 ([ETS subject test changes page](https://www.ets.org/gre/score-users/subject-test-changes.html), accessed 2026-10-12). **[INFER]** Relevant only if a specific applied-physics program still requires it — increasingly rare, and the handoff doc's target programs (engineering/bioengineering/earth-science departments) are unlikely to require a Physics Subject Test.

**TOEFL iBT:**
- **[FACT]** Taiwan fee figure found in a third-party aggregator citing Sept 2026 ETS data: ~**US$260**; an NTU Language Center notice from Feb 2025 lists US$245 + possible US$40 late fee — **figures are inconsistent across sources and should be confirmed directly on the ETS registration page before paying** ([search synthesis], accessed 2026-10-12). No official ETS Taiwan-specific fee page was retrieved directly in this research.
- **[FACT]** Test centers exist in Taipei, Taoyuan, Taichung, Tainan, Kaohsiung at universities and private language centers ([NTU LTTC iBT registration notice](https://www.lttc.ntu.edu.tw/en/iBT_Registration_notice), accessed 2026-10-12, among others).

**IELTS:**
- **[FACT]** Two providers operate in Taiwan: **IDP** (30+ test sites / 120+ registration sites across 6 cities — Taipei, Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Taichung, Tainan, Kaohsiung) and **British Council** (Taipei exam office) ([IDP Taiwan](https://ielts.sit.idp.com/taiwan/about/why-choose-idp-ielts/en-gb), accessed 2026-10-12). Results typically in ~13 days (3–5 for computer-delivered).

**[INFER] English test waiver policies**, not independently verified per program but consistent across the sources reviewed: an applicant with an undergraduate or graduate degree instructed substantially in English, or who has lived/studied in an English-speaking country for a threshold period (commonly 2+ years), can often get an English-test waiver — but **NTU/NYCU instruction in Taiwan is primarily Chinese-medium**, so this waiver route likely does **not** apply by default; check each program's specific waiver criteria (some waive based on test scores like a sufficiently high TOEFL from years prior, or based on "medium of instruction" letters from the university registrar).

### 1.7 Application fee and fee waivers
**[FACT]** US programs commonly restrict or exclude fee waivers for international applicants:
- University of Virginia and Oregon State: **international applicants are not eligible** for standard fee waivers ([search synthesis citing UVA/OSU grad admissions], accessed 2026-10-12).
- MIT: will consider international applicants for a waiver **only** if they are currently attending a US college and participated in specific graduate-research-prep programs — **not** for financial hardship alone ([MIT OGE fee waiver page](https://oge.mit.edu/graduate-admissions/applications/application-fee-waiver), accessed 2026-10-12).
- Some schools (Univ. of Oregon) keep a small discretionary pool for international applicants with significant need, gated by program nomination.
**[INFER]** Net practical implication: **budget for full application fees** (commonly US$75–125 per US program) as the default assumption; fee waivers are the exception for international applicants, not the rule, and depend on departmental discretion rather than a general policy.

## 2. Timeline/deadline windows for Fall 2027 admission

### 2.1 United States
**[FACT]** PhD deadlines for Fall 2027 cluster in **November–December 2026**, consistent with the research brief's assumption:
- UC Berkeley Mechanical Engineering PhD: **December 1, 2026** ([Berkeley grad division program page](https://grad.berkeley.edu/program/mechanical-engineering/), accessed 2026-10-12).
- Vanderbilt Engineering PhD: recommended **December 15, 2026**, final acceptance through **January 8, 2027**; fee-waiver requests must come in by **December 1, 2026** ([Vanderbilt engineering grad admissions](https://engineering.vanderbilt.edu/graduate-admissions/), accessed 2026-10-12).
**[INFER]** This Nov–Dec clustering is standard for US STEM PhD programs generally (consistent with prior-knowledge baseline), but **exceptions exist** — e.g., University of Denver's engineering PhDs run a Feb/June priority/final deadline structure for the 2027–28 cycle, well outside the Nov–Dec window ([search result citing du.edu admission-steps pages], accessed 2026-10-12). **Do not assume Nov–Dec for every program — check each one.**

### 2.2 Europe — highly non-uniform, three different models
**[FACT/INFER — synthesized, not a single source]** Unlike the US, Europe doesn't have one deadline season because "apply to a PhD" means different things depending on country and program type:

1. **UK** — PhD admission is often **not deadline-bound** for self-funded/direct applications (rolling, apply when ready, offer tied to supervisor availability), but **funded studentships** (UKRI/research-council-funded, or tied to a specific grant) carry hard deadlines, often **Dec–Feb** for Oct/Fall-equivalent starts the following year. Imperial College's process (a target institution in the handoff doc) requires identifying and contacting a supervisor *before* applying for a self-proposed project, or responding to an advertised funded studentship with its own deadline ([Imperial Design Engineering PhD apply page](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/design-engineering/study/phd/applying-for-a-phd-in-design-engineering/); [Imperial Bioengineering PhD apply page](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/engineering/departments/bioengineering/study/postgraduate-research/doctoral/apply/), both accessed 2026-10-12). **[INFER]** Net effect: there is no generic "UK PhD deadline" — it's set by the specific funding instrument and supervisor, discoverable only once a program/PI is chosen.

2. **Switzerland** (e.g., ETH Zürich) — **[FACT]** Doctoral admission formally requires a professor's **written agreement to supervise** before the university-level admission process even starts; there is no fixed annual deadline in the US sense — the clock starts when a supervisor agrees ([ETH doctoral ordinance, via search synthesis], accessed 2026-10-12). Matriculation fee: CHF 150 for holders of a non-ETH/EPFL foreign Master's degree. **[INFER]** This means for Switzerland, "the deadline" is really "securing a supervisor's agreement," which the applicant should budget months for — DAAD's own guidance (below) suggests 6–12 months lead time for the equivalent step in Germany, likely a reasonable analogy for Switzerland.

3. **Germany** — **[FACT]** DAAD (the main funding body) deadlines are typically **October 31** for the main annual cycle of its doctoral research grants, with internal host-university deadlines sometimes earlier (e.g., a cited internal UC Irvine deadline of Sept 27 for a DAAD-nominee process) ([search synthesis of DAAD/university pages], accessed 2026-10-12 — note: these are prior-cycle dates reported by university pages, not confirmed against DAAD's current-cycle page directly). **[FACT]** DAAD explicitly expects the applicant to **already have identified and contacted a prospective German supervisor** (or secured acceptance into a structured program) before applying — "proof of contact with a German professor" is part of the application ([DAAD "How to find a PhD supervisor" guide](https://www.daad.de/medien/deutschland/stipendien/formulare/gerss-how-to-find-a-phd-supervisor.pdf), accessed 2026-10-12).

**[INFER — important]** The practical consequence for the applicant's "Europe 5–6 programs" plan: **the Europe half of the application timeline is front-loaded relative to the US half.** Direct professor contact and (for Germany) securing agreement needs to start well before the US Nov–Dec deadlines, likely **spring–summer 2026** for Fall 2027 — this is consistent with, and reinforces, the handoff doc's existing emphasis on finding POIs early and the "choose deliberately in March [2027]" fallback framing, but note that if direct contact + agreement-securing in Europe is the bottleneck, it needs to start *before* March 2027 for a Fall 2027 start, not after.

## 3. Taiwan-specific procedural points

### 3.1 Transcript/diploma authentication and translation
**[FACT]** For documents **issued in Taiwan being used abroad**, Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs Bureau of Consular Affairs runs a diploma/transcript authentication process (notarize at a District Court/Notary Public Office, then authenticate at the Bureau of Consular Affairs), or the applicant can have the school send the original documents directly to that office ([ROC-Taiwan.org authentication requirements PDF](https://www.roc-taiwan.org/uploads/sites/109/2018/01/Requirements-for-authentication-of-Diploma-Transcript-Records.pdf), accessed 2026-10-12). **[INFER]** This formal MOFA-authentication route is typically needed for **visa/immigration purposes or for use by a foreign government office**, not necessarily for university *admission* (which usually accepts a university-issued English transcript directly) — confirm per receiving country.

**[FACT]** For **Germany specifically**: Germany has no formal diplomatic relations with Taiwan, but the German Institute Taipei (its de facto embassy) can legalize some official Taiwanese public documents; it explicitly states it does **not** legalize notarized copies (originals only) and does **not** legalize "documents of a private nature" — whether an academic transcript/diploma counts as a document the Institute will legalize is **not confirmed** in these sources, and the Institute's own guidance says to check with the destination German authority first ([German Institute Taipei legalization page](https://taipei.diplo.de/tw-en/service/1695492-1695492), accessed 2026-10-12). **This is a genuine open point requiring direct confirmation with the German Institute Taipei or the specific receiving university/visa office.**

### 3.2 Financial documentation for visa purposes
**[FACT]** Both UK and German student-visa processes require proof of funds at specific, published monthly rates (see §4), which a Taiwanese applicant would typically satisfy via a bank statement, parental sponsorship letter, or a blocked account (Germany) — no Taiwan-specific exception was found; Taiwan is **not** on the UK's "differential evidence" exempt-country list (that list includes Australia, Canada, Germany among others — [GOV.UK student visa financial evidence summary via search], accessed 2026-10-12), so a Taiwanese applicant **does** need to provide full financial evidence for a UK visa, unlike nationals of some other countries.

### 3.3 Standardized test logistics in Taiwan
**[FACT]** TOEFL iBT, IELTS (via both IDP and British Council), and GRE are all administered in Taiwan (Taipei/Taoyuan/Taichung/Tainan/Kaohsiung for TOEFL and IELTS). **[INFER]** No unusual Taiwan-specific scheduling constraint was found beyond standard advice to book early in the Oct–Dec window, since that's peak season for Fall-admission test-takers worldwide, not a Taiwan-only issue.

## 4. Visa procedure basics once admitted

### 4.1 United States — F-1 / SEVIS / I-20
**[FACT]** Sequence, per DHS's Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP) materials ([Study in the States "F-1 Postsecondary" guide](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/guide/f-1/f-1-postsecondary), accessed 2026-10-12; [SEVP International Student Life Cycle 2022/2025 PDF](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/assets/SEVP_InternationalStudentLifecycle_2022.pdf)):
1. School admits the student; its Designated School Official (DSO) creates a SEVIS record and issues **Form I-20**.
2. Student pays the **I-901 SEVIS fee** via FMJfee.com (required before the visa interview).
3. Student applies for the **F-1 visa** at a US embassy/consulate (AIT in Taipei, for a Taiwanese applicant), including an **in-person interview**.
4. Student may enter the US **up to 30 days before** the program start date on the I-20.
5. Student must report to the DSO on/before the program start date; maintain full-time enrollment; work only with prior authorization (on-campus, CPT, OPT).
6. After program completion, **60 days** to depart the US (unless on approved OPT or transferring status).
**[INFER]** No Taiwan-specific wrinkle beyond the standard AIT visa-interview wait-time variability (not itself researched here — AIT interview wait times fluctuate and should be checked closer to the time via the State Department's visa-wait-time page).

### 4.2 United Kingdom — Student visa
**[FACT]** ([GOV.UK Student visa page](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa) and [GOV.UK Student visa money page](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money), both accessed 2026-10-12):
- Apply **up to 6 months before** course start if applying from outside the UK; decision typically **~3 weeks**.
- Requires: a confirmed offer from a licensed student sponsor (**CAS** — Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies), proof of English proficiency, proof of identity, financial evidence.
- **Financial evidence**: must show funds for course fees (per CAS) **plus** living costs — **£1,529/month in London or £1,171/month outside London**, for up to 9 months, **held for 28 consecutive days**, with that 28-day period ending within 31 days of the application date. Taiwan is not on the exempt-country list, so this applies in full.
- **Fee**: £558 (from outside the UK), plus the **Immigration Health Surcharge** (amount depends on visa length, not given on the page fetched).
- Can enter the UK up to 1 month early for courses longer than 6 months.

### 4.3 Germany — National (study) visa
**[FACT]** ([German Institute Taipei visa checklist pages, taipei.diplo.de, accessed 2026-10-12]):
- Taiwanese passport holders are **not** visa-exempt for German study — must obtain the national visa before travel.
- Apply **in person** at the German Institute Taipei (appointment required); biometrics and photo taken on-site; processing continues after submission while the Institute awaits approval from the relevant German *Ausländerbehörde* (foreigner authority) — **this step is a known bottleneck/variable-duration step**, not a fixed number of weeks in the sources found.
- Required: valid passport, university admission letter, proof of language proficiency (IELTS/TOEFL/TOEIC for English-taught programs; TestDaF/DSH/Goethe-Zertifikat for German-taught), and **proof of finances of at least €992/month for the first 12 months** (typically via a blocked account — "Sperrkonto") — **verify current figure, as these are revised periodically.**
- Visa fee: €75.
- After arrival, the visa must be **converted to a residence permit** (Aufenthaltserlaubnis) at the local immigration office — a second, in-country step.

### 4.4 Switzerland — National Type D visa
**[FACT]** ([Trade Office of Swiss Industries Taipei student-visa PDF](https://www.eda.admin.ch/dam/countries/countries-content/taiwan-taipei/en/Student-Visa_EN.pdf); general Swiss-university guidance pages, accessed 2026-10-12):
- Non-EU/EFTA students staying >90 days need the **Type D national visa**, applied for **in person**, by appointment, at the Swiss representation in the applicant's country of residence (for Taiwan: the Trade Office of Swiss Industries, acting as the de facto Swiss representation).
- The application is forwarded to the **cantonal migration office** for approval — university guidance (ZHAW) warns the **whole process can take up to ~3 months**; another estimate gives 8–12 weeks.
- Typical documents: admission letter, proof of finances, proof of prior qualifications; an oral language test may be requested by some missions.
**[INFER]** Of the four jurisdictions compared, Switzerland's visa step appears to have the least standardized/published timeline among official sources — budgeting the longer (3-month) estimate is the safer assumption.

## 5. Direct contact with professors of interest (POI) — US vs. Europe norms

**[FACT]** **United States**: contact is generally optional and sometimes actively discouraged, decided case by case by department/program, not a national norm:
- UT Austin CS FAQ: applicants are **not required** to contact faculty outside the application; "many professors are not able to respond to prospective student inquiries and some professors ask that prospective students do not contact them directly" ([UT Austin CS FAQ](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/faq/3741), accessed 2026-10-12).
- Counter-example: Stony Brook's Ecology & Evolution PhD page **strongly advises** contacting faculty before applying ([Stony Brook EcoEvo PhD admissions](https://stonybrook.edu/ecoevo/academic-programs/phd-admissions.html), accessed 2026-10-12).
- **[INFER]** US admissions in STEM are frequently **committee-based** rather than single-PI-based, which structurally reduces (but does not eliminate) the value/necessity of pre-admission PI contact — this matches the handoff doc's existing framing. Net: **check each program's own instructions; default to "contact is optional, and a short, specific email close to the deadline is low-risk," per program-level signals, not a blanket rule.**

**[FACT]** **Europe** — contact is frequently a structural *prerequisite*, not a courtesy, because many PhD positions are tied to a specific grant/project or because formal admission requires a named, agreeing supervisor:
- **Switzerland (ETH)**: the admissions procedure can only be *initiated* once "a professor has agreed in writing to supervise the doctoral thesis" — admission is not possible without this ([ETH doctoral ordinance, via search synthesis], accessed 2026-10-12).
- **Germany (DAAD)**: applicants are expected to have **already contacted** a prospective German supervisor and obtained proof of that contact (an invitation letter, or documented correspondence) as part of the application package — DAAD's own guide gives concrete tactics (cold-emailing program coordinators with a short research proposal, using the GERiT database) ([DAAD "How to find a PhD supervisor" guide](https://www.daad.de/medien/deutschland/stipendien/formulare/gerss-how-to-find-a-phd-supervisor.pdf), accessed 2026-10-12).
- **UK (Imperial, as example)**: for a self-proposed project, the applicant is explicitly told to "discuss your research idea with a potential supervisor before you apply"; for funded studentships, the applicant responds to an advertisement that already names the supervisor and project ([Imperial Design Engineering PhD apply page](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/design-engineering/study/phd/applying-for-a-phd-in-design-engineering/), accessed 2026-10-12).

**[INFER — the key structural difference]**: in the US, the "unit" being admitted is typically the *student*, evaluated by a committee, with the advisor matched after admission (varies by program — some are more PI-driven, e.g. many engineering PhDs do ask applicants to name a POI). In Switzerland/Germany/UK (for funded or self-proposed PhDs), the "unit" being admitted is often the *student-plus-project-plus-supervisor triad*, and the supervisor's agreement is a **gating step that must happen before or during the application**, not after. This directly supports the handoff doc's existing plan to weight Europe applications toward POI contact and funding/position fit, and US applications toward the general SOP/committee process — this research simply confirms that structural asymmetry against official sources rather than assuming it.

## 6. Open questions (depend on the specific schools eventually chosen)

These cannot be resolved generically — they require the actual target list (the handoff doc's "10–12 programs, frozen" step) to answer:

1. **Exact GRE/TOEFL/IELTS requirement and minimum scores** per target department — confirmed as genuinely non-uniform even within one university; must be checked program-by-program, not assumed from a university-wide page.
2. **Whether a WES/ECE-style credential evaluation is required** in addition to the NTU/NYCU-issued English transcript, for each specific US program — this adds real lead time (weeks) if required and not anticipated.
3. **Whether the German Institute Taipei will legalize a Taiwanese diploma/transcript**, and whether the receiving German authority even requires legalization for the specific visa/admission path chosen (self-funded vs. DAAD-funded vs. structured program) — flagged above as genuinely unresolved in this research; needs a direct query to the Institute or the specific German host university once one is targeted.
4. **UK funded-studentship deadlines** are tied to the specific funding call (e.g., a UKRI DTP, an individual PI's grant) and cannot be generalized — only knowable once specific Imperial/other-UK supervisors and funding routes are identified.
5. **Current-cycle DAAD deadline** (the Oct 31 figure found is corroborated across several prior cycles but was not confirmed against DAAD's live Fall-2027-cycle page) — reconfirm directly on daad.de closer to the application window.
6. **Swiss visa processing time** for the specific canton (Zürich for ETH) — official sources gave a range (8–12 weeks to "up to 3 months"); cantonal variation was not resolved here.
7. **Whether a specific target program expects/requires POI contact** is binary and program-specific (shown directly above: UT Austin discourages it, Stony Brook requires it, Imperial/ETH/DAAD effectively require it) — this is the single biggest "it depends" in the whole procedure and must be checked per program, not assumed from field or country alone.
8. **Fee-waiver eligibility** for international applicants is discretionary and program-specific (MIT, UVA, Oregon State, Vanderbilt all differ) — worth a direct, low-cost email query to each target program's admissions office once the list is frozen, since several explicitly route this through departmental discretion rather than a published policy.
